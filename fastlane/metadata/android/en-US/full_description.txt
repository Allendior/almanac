Almanac is a private, offline-first record of intentional self-portraits, meant to be
looked back on years later.

It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics, no cloud requirement, and no social
features. It declares only the Camera, biometric-lock, and notification permissions —
no internet access, no location, no broad storage access. Every photograph and every
piece of metadata stays in the app's private storage on your device.

Core ideas:

* At least one portrait a day, and as many more as you actually take. Nothing is
  replaced or discarded to make room for another; every portrait is kept,
  individually. Taken with the in-app camera only — there is no photo picker and no
  gallery import, so a portrait can be taken, never chosen from elsewhere.
* The saved photograph is never altered. No filters, no beautifying, no cropping. An
  optional on-screen framing guide (a faint previous-portrait ghost, or a simple grid)
  helps you line up the shot, but is never part of the saved file. An optional torch
  is available on cameras that actually have one.
* A missed day is never punished. There are no streaks, no completion percentages, and
  no red marks on the calendar. There is one optional daily reminder, on by default —
  it fires at most once a day, only if today has no portrait yet, and is a single
  switch away from off, permanently.
* Follows your system's light or dark setting automatically.
* Browse your archive by calendar (with a count badge on days with more than one
  portrait), by a scrolling timeline, or compare any two dates — or any two portraits
  from the same day — side by side to see how much time has passed.
* A page of optional, unenforced tips for a portrait that compares well across years —
  never shown unprompted, and nothing about it is checked or scored.
* An optional biometric app lock, off by default.
* Explicit export to a plain ZIP (your original photographs plus a readable JSON
  index) via the system file picker, and matching import with full validation, a
  visible report, and no risk of overwriting what is already in your archive.

Optional self-hosted backup to your own home server is planned as a future,
separately-approved feature. It will remain off by default, foreground-only, and will
never be a requirement for the app to work — and it will never involve any third
party's server.
